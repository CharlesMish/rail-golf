# The Next Playgrounds — a first visit

Three experiments invite different kinds of attention. There is no required
order, route or score. Start with whichever question sounds appealing.

Open **`/lab/playgrounds`** on the pinned preview linked in the integration
draft PR and director handoff message. Each page displays its build identity.
The same build contains all three experiments.

## Two-Shadow Theatre

**A visual instrument made from real flights.** The front and wing panes show
the same ball from perpendicular directions. The small stage plan helps you
relate the two views. These are schematic projections, not a 3D lighting effect.

Choose aim, elevation and power, then fire. Watch how one change reads in both
views. The upright flat and sloping rostrum offer different possibilities, but
an open flight is a valid composition too.

Keep a line that suggests another. Compare chosen lines and move the shared
time cursor to inspect the same moment in both views. The scale stays fixed;
framing choices apply to the views rather than fitting each shot independently.
Restore prepares the original launch. Fire again to run the physics.

The shelf holds six chosen lines. Reset launch keeps them. Remove is explicit,
and a full shelf does not overwrite an older line. A storage warning means you
should use Download if you want to retain a record.

A useful question: **Can two lines resemble each other from one direction while
telling different stories from the other?**

Route: `/lab/playgrounds-theatre`.

## Gallery Cistern

**Explore a room with several possible destinations.** Begin in Survey and look
for the high and low galleries, lower receiving area and connected side chamber.
The dark floor and launch pier end a shot. The other solid architecture can
sustain it; leaving the authored bounds also ends the attempt.

Choose a rail position, aim, elevation and power. Pick a region you are curious
about, watch what happens, then change one thing. A miss can reveal a different
part of the room worth visiting next.

Keep a line when you want to return to its setup. Restore prepares its original
conditions; it does not fire for you. Some shots remain in the room a long time.
You do not need to treat a long sequence as a good one.

A useful question: **After one interesting route, is there another part of the
room—or another way back to the same place—you want to try?**

Route: `/lab/playgrounds-architecture`.

## Guest Stone

**A persistent multi-shot experiment.** The stone is moved by actual projectile
impacts and stays in its new resting position. The north recess and eastern
alcove are suggestions for a project, not compulsory targets. You may prefer to
turn the stone or use its new face in another line.

Aim deliberately at the stone or a bank that could reach it. The pavement ends
the ball's flight. Continue watching: the stone may still be moving. The next
shot becomes available after it has settled.

The controls have separate purposes:

- **Next shot:** keep the settled garden and prepare another launch.
- **Undo last blow:** restore the stone and launch to before the last attempt.
- **Reset garden:** put the stone back at its authored starting position; kept
  blows remain separate.
- **Keep this blow:** save one of four chosen attempts with its starting and
  settled arrangements.
- **Restore before:** prepare the saved starting state to repeat a blow.
- **Use result:** prepare the saved settled state to continue the project.

Restore and Use result always require a new manual shot. If the stone cannot
settle safely, Fire stays blocked and Undo/Reset lets you recover. No hidden
movement finishes the delivery for you.

This is a more deliberate aiming task than the Cistern. A clean miss leaves the
stone alone. That distinction is part of what the experiment is testing.

A useful question: **Would you willingly spend this shot making the next one
possible?**

Route: `/lab/playgrounds-persistence`.

## What feedback would help

A few observations are more useful than completing a list of fixtures:

- What did you want the next shot to do?
- What made that intention easy or hard to see and control?
- Did a Keep become useful later?
- Did the experiment suggest something else after its first interesting result?

Stop whenever a place stops giving you a reason to continue. That is useful
feedback. The physical proofs and numerical solutions are separate from this
guide, so there is no need to look them up before exploring.

The Cistern and Guest Stone need a WebGL-enabled browser. Theatre uses SVG to
show actual Havok results and can run without WebGL. Theatre was checked in a
desktop browser and a 390-pixel-wide frame. The 3D scenes could not be rendered
in the research browser, so camera legibility and physical-phone performance
remain part of your playtest rather than established results.
