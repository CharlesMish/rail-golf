# Chime Terraces — three worlds

Open them from the range list. Chime Terraces, Chime Fan, and Chime Garden sit together. Each one is a fixed set of pieces. Origin, yaw, elevation, and power can move inside the rail stops. There is no circle and no score. The line ends on first ground, or when the ball leaves the yard.

The note line under a shot names what sounded, in order. A small step on a slider is the way to listen: the same pieces often answer again, a little longer or a little shorter.

## Chime Terraces

Three timber ramps stand in front of the tee. Behind them, three rows of metal plates rise and lean back toward the tee. A path that moves down a column changes note. The pieces stay where they are.

Survey shows the three heights from the side. The printed aim is one place to start. Other aims inside the stops reach other plates.

## Chime Fan

Three banks stand across the clearing, and plates hang above the far side of each one. Aim left, ahead, or right. The pieces stay where they are.

The aim that opens the world rings one bank. A different side rings a different bank. Raising the aim can carry the ball over a bank and on into the plates and the piece behind them.

The bank sound control is the existing timber voice. Metal plates and the far banks stay the long metal voice. Sound does not change the flight. Replay plays the last shot again.

## Chime Garden

Four timber banks and eight metal plates stand in a loose garden. Aim into different parts of the clearing. The ball can glance from one surface into another. The pieces stay where they are.

Some aims ring nothing. A shot that rings one surface can be walked, a small step at a time, until more surfaces answer. The opening aim is one place to start.

The bank sound control is the same timber voice as on the fan. Metal plates stay ordinary metal. Sound does not change the flight.
