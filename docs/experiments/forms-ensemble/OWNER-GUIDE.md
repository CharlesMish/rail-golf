# Architectural Forms & Ensemble — owner play guide

Start at `/lab/forms-ensemble` in the pinned preview recorded in the draft PR.
The two activities are independent. Nothing here replaces production or the
previous pinned experiments. Numerical recipes live in separate technical files.

## Exchange Yard

Choose **Canopy**, the new physical layout. Revisit a familiar Forecourt journey,
then try the Freight dock facing back through the district. The broad maintenance
roof beside the crossing is another place to receive the ball. Its sloping crown
and open passage offer different approaches. Try a small change after something
catches your attention; a short surprising departure is as useful as a long trip.

The usual aim, elevation, power, station and camera controls remain. Qualified
redirect rings still mark substantial physical bounces. Raised architecture is
physical; the dark street ends the shot. **About the yard & controls** contains
the physical-version selector. **Refined** restores the prior Forecourt/Freight
layout; **Original** restores Forecourt/Service. Each has its own line shelf.

The useful question is whether you deliberately return to the new region from
either station, or whether it merely adds an incidental bounce. Its crown is
visibly faceted, and its underside is flat: this is a solid barrel-roof canopy,
not a hollow arch. Please also notice whether the camera keeps its relationships
understandable. The available geometric checks cannot establish visual comfort.

## Counterline Ensemble

Discover a phrase in any of the four musical worlds and **Keep** it. Choose it
as Companion 1. Find and Keep another phrase, perhaps in another world, and
choose Companion 2. You can change the live world while keeping both selections.
Selecting a companion never restores its physical environment.

Fire normally. Both recordings play once beside the new physical shot, from
their original contact timings. Try a sparse answer as well as a busy one.
Remove one companion for ordinary Counterline, or both for solo play.

Each contribution has mute and level controls. New layers start at 30%, with
45% available. Set levels before Fire; they remain fixed during playback. Mute
cuts that layer's tails, and unmute plays only its future notes. A natural shot
ending lets the remaining music finish. Stop or Retry cancels it.

**Keep** retains the new physical line. **Save ensemble** becomes available after
an uninterrupted complete musical performance and retains all three independent
records and settings. There are four separate ensemble slots in addition to the
four Keeps. Saved ensembles retain their source copies if a Keep is removed.
Loading is silent. **Audition** is explicitly recorded playback of all parts;
it creates no projectile. Download material you want to retain before deletion.
Old physical lines and Counterline pairs remain supported.

Does the second companion suggest a different next shot, or fill the space you
wanted to play into? Is one world useful for a particular answer? These are owner
listening questions; the measured timing and peaks cannot answer them.

## Roof comparison, only if useful

The roof change is **not yet a verified repair**. The research browser cannot
render WebGL. If you still see black patches, stripes, missing faces or flicker,
open **Compare roof rendering** from the Yard's About section. The separate
`/lab/roof-comparison` page keeps diagnostics out of ordinary play.

Compare PR86 and Candidate at the same view, first in the full yard and then
with the isolated hall. Above, eave and gable views plus slow orbit are useful.
Bias-only, filter-only, reception-off, two-sided and cap-removal variants each
isolate one hypothesis. A result that changes only at an eave is different from
one that removes stripes across the entire slope. There are no shots or Keeps
in this diagnostic. The [roof guide](roof-owner.md) explains what each contrast
can and cannot establish.
