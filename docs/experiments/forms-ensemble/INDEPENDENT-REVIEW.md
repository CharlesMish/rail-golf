## Architecture: retain the place, qualify the curve

The maintenance canopy occupies the verified opposite side of the signal house. Its single convex roof uses the same faceted vertices for rendering and the Havok hull. Four matching box piers leave room underneath. The soffit is flat: this is a barrel-crowned solid prism, not a hollow arch or a smooth visual roof concealing a flat collision plane.

The independent topology probe finds 22 canonical vertices, 60 edges and 40 triangles, with two incident triangles at every edge, Euler characteristic two, outward flat normals and all vertices inside the same supporting planes used by camera constraints. All original 20 architectural solid position/normal/index buffers remain exact. The addition costs five static bodies; v3 rejects the v2 environment identity. The old physical worlds remain separately selectable.

Five independently replayed actual-Havok cases, each with the six ordinary neighboring half-degree/one-percentage-point controls, support a bounded design judgment:

| Relationship | Neighbors retaining relationship | Simpler flat-control implication |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Forecourt canopy to hall | 6/6 | Same launch reaches canopy then street, without hall |
| Forecourt canopy to crossing | 4/6 | Same launch reaches canopy then street |
| Freight direct canopy | 6/6 | Reception itself is the main opportunity |
| Freight roof/crossing/canopy | 6/6 | Flat canopy preserves the central contact sequence |
| Freight longer transfer | 1/6 | Fragile; flat preserves the central sequence too |

The flat control covers slightly more of the author's sampled launch grid. Therefore the reason to keep the barrel crown is its different Forecourt departure and coherent silhouette, not maximum accessibility count. Freight gains a useful destination from the receiver's location; it does not by itself demonstrate a need for curvature. The long transfer should remain an optional discovery, not the owner entry recipe.

The independent contact packet retains actual normals, times and whole-step incoming/outgoing velocity brackets. Most selected contacts align with a face; edge callbacks can differ. The author's wider edge sweep correctly reports two non-face normals rather than pretending the faceted hull has smooth curvature. Neither report attributes a whole-step velocity change to one isolated impulse.

The v3-only camera adjustment and expanded-solid clipping are appropriately scoped. Its geometric audits support visibility and collision avoidance; they cannot establish comfort or architectural readability without rendering. The owner should decide whether the canopy becomes a deliberate destination or merely an obligatory extra bounce after the established Freight transfer.

## Roof: useful diagnostic isolation, unresolved pixels

The candidate changes shadow filtering/bias only through the Exchange Yard builder. It preserves the roof buffers, collider construction, culling, receivers and casters. No global production shadow setting changes. The comparator separates original blurred ESM, bias-only, filter-only, the combined candidate, roof reception, two-sided material and render-only building-cap removal. Full-yard context matters because a roof-only shadow frustum does not reproduce the district's shadow conditions.

The reviewed comparator disposes/recreates scenes for recipe/scope changes, repositions an existing camera for view changes, guards asynchronous teardown, and retains explanatory controls when WebGL fails. Its local scrolling does not alter historical full-screen routes. It exposes the actual fallback filter rather than promising PCF on unsupported shadow samplers.

Exact coplanarity of roof bases and building caps is a genuine inspection target, but cannot explain every reported striped slope or establish the cause alone. The independent combined test run includes 23 full before/after Havok histories and original roof buffers; all remain exact. That proves presentation isolation. It does **not** prove the owner's flicker, black patches or missing faces are fixed. Keep the comparison available and ask for above/eave/gable views on a working renderer before promoting the candidate to a resolved defect.

## Ensemble: source independence and bounded lifecycle

The additive v1 ensemble keeps three independent physical records and three explicit acoustic policies/settings. Null historical acoustics remain null, so a previously uncaptured timbre is not silently invented. Only one projectile is created. The selected source snapshots survive live-world changes; restoring their physical setup is a separate action. The separate four-arrangement library embeds complete sources, so removing a Keep does not erase an arrangement. The third recorded source is available for subsequent accompaniment.

The review challenged the draft's centered capacity count, active gain mutation, storage envelope limit and source-restore policy. The reviewed implementation uses a true 2.4-second sliding window, refuses level changes during playback, cuts only the muted layer, aligns read/write limits, and passes the selected source's explicit policy on Restore. Imported old pairs and ordinary physical lines retain their previous formats. A live capacity overflow interrupts explicitly; it does not silently omit physical notes. That remains a bounded experimental limitation, not universal support for all dense source combinations.

The independent probe preserves one physical line simultaneously as historical canonical playback and captured H playback, round-trips both policies, and verifies corrupt/concurrently modified storage remains untouched. Its clock run schedules ten recorded/live attacks at their original launch-relative targets with separate layer gains, refuses active level edits, reaches natural completion with zero voices/timers and leaves source bytes unchanged. The rolling-window regression counts three separated 18-attack clusters as a maximum of 36, not 54.

The source review of completed-performance retention confirms natural completion clears the pending flag; explicit Stop/focus/audio interruption invalidates a pending live combination; stopping an audition of an already recorded arrangement preserves that historical record. Save and download remain unavailable while the original musical performance is active. The final review found that current-audition access during companion tails could promote a provisional record before Stop cleared it. The reviewed final guard disables current audition while audio is active and refuses the action while a live performance is pending. An already recorded arrangement remains independently auditionable after Stop.

The author's 252 complete physical-payload comparisons and 14 focused ensemble tests supplement this review. Their simulated frame partitions are not browser frame rates, and the fake audio context is not listening evidence. The inherited Descending construction-history/seam sensitivity remains disclosed. Musical quality, perceived balance, output latency and a phone's sustainable voice budget require device/owner evidence.

## Reproduction and decision

The combined independent run passed 49 tests across the ensemble, old pair/repertoire, architectural form, old architecture/refinement and roof-presentation suites. After the completed-performance followups, the 14 ensemble tests also passed again; the final UI-only audition guard was inspected directly. The two review probes are separately reproducible:

- `node scripts/forms/review-architecture.mjs` writes `review-architecture.json` using actual 120 Hz Havok. Numerical shot settings are confined to that technical appendix.
- `node scripts/forms/review-ensemble.mjs` writes `review-ensemble.json` using a stub scheduling clock, storage and real archived physical source records.

Neither probe establishes rendered frame rate, GPU cleanup, physical-phone performance or enjoyment. Repository-wide type/lint/build/Foundry/full-suite results and pinned publication SHAs belong to the director handoff.

Retain these two candidates for owner play. For future authoring, a bounded faceted convex receiving form is more defensible than a general geometry editor; independent musical sources are more useful than immediately adding a fourth layer. Revise that judgment if the canopy is rarely approached intentionally, if ensemble play is mostly passive replay, or if listening shows that additional source density obscures the player's new physical phrase.
