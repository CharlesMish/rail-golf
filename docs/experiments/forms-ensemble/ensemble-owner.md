# Play with your ensemble

Begin in any of the four musical worlds. Discover a phrase and Keep it. Choose
it as Companion 1. Change the live world and find another phrase; Keep that as
Companion 2. The two selections travel with you when you change the live world.
Select None to remove either companion. Both empty means solo play.

Aim and Fire normally. Only the new ball is physical. Both remembered phrases
play once from their original launch-relative contact timings. They can end
before or after your new shot; let the combination finish if you want its tails.

Each contribution has a mute and a level. New layers begin at 30% for ensemble
headroom; up to 45% is available. Set levels before Fire. During playback,
levels remain fixed; mute is available and cuts that layer's ringing tails.
Unmuting plays only subsequent notes. Stop or Retry cancels the whole pairing.
Nothing resumes after navigating, reloading or returning from lost focus.

Keep preserves the new physical line. Save ensemble preserves the independent
source recordings and the fixed playback settings. Changing mute during a
performance leaves the physical line intact but prevents saving a falsely fixed
ensemble. There are four Keep slots and four separate ensemble slots. An
ensemble retains its own source copies even if you later remove their Keeps.

Choose companions on a saved ensemble loads silently without changing the live
world or aim. Audition explicitly loads that arrangement and plays **all parts
as recordings**, with no new projectile. Its third contribution is the previous
live line. The ordinary current-shot display continues to identify actual play.
Download the selected ensemble or your Keeps before deleting anything you want
to retain. Imports stay silent; old Counterline pairs and physical lines remain
supported. A source's Restore physical setup action deliberately changes the
live world, separately from selecting it as musical accompaniment.

Descending's selected material offers spaced strikes with long timber tails;
Terraces can offer an early strike followed by a clustered answer. Their pitches
share the existing register. Treat those as possibilities to discover, not fixed
instrument roles. A single well-timed note may answer the other two phrases more
usefully than another long sequence.

Unsupported density or a missed audio deadline stops explicitly. The research
browser's rendering and listening limitations are recorded separately. The
question for owner play is whether a second companion suggests a useful new
physical gesture, or simply fills too much of the space.
