# Exchange Yard & Counterline — play guide

Start at `/lab/refinement` on the pinned preview in the draft PR. The publication
ledger links the final candidate and both independent checkpoints. Nothing here
has been merged or deployed to production.

## Revisit Exchange Yard

Start from **Forecourt** and revisit a journey you enjoyed. Aim and elevation have
sliders and small nudge buttons. The orange power slider and value are visible;
**Fire** uses that setting. **Timed hold** remains available if you prefer charging.
You can also drag the scene to aim. **Next shot / Retry** prepares another attempt.

Then choose **Freight dock**. You are looking back through the same district from
a modestly raised starting point. Consider what could receive a descending line,
or where a roof might send the ball next. No new district or hidden receiver was
added. Changing the station is a choice of approach, not a progression reward.

**Launch view**, **Survey** and **Follow ball** change how you watch. **Stop** ends
the current attempt. Dark street is terminal ground; raised architecture is
physical. Fine tracks, windows and masonry are surface paint. The occasional
glowing rings mark qualified physical redirects, with no compulsory objective.

Keep an interesting completed line before moving on. The shelf, Restore and
detailed records are in separate disclosures. Under **About the yard & controls**,
**Physical yard** switches to the original Forecourt/Service arrangement with the
same repaired roofs and new controls. Its shelf is separate. The **Original
preview** link also preserves the complete PR84 interface for comparison.

Useful observations: Does Freight suggest a different next shot? Are ordinary
controls immediately understandable? Do the roof faces look solid from both
sides, and do their lighting and shadows make sense? Is the narrower desktop
scene or the portrait split comfortable? Our research browser could not render
WebGL, so your visual observations remain particularly useful.

## Find a phrase, then answer it

**Descending Resonance** favors separated strikes and falling phrases.
**Chime Terraces** offers a larger spread of plates, including close clusters and
ringing combinations. Both retain their original authored geometry and notes.
Each opens on a clearly labelled, approachable starting setup from its existing
repertoire; **Original opening** is available for comparison. The two smaller
workshops remain freely selectable.

Start solo. Fire, listen, and Keep something that interests you. Select it as a
companion, change the next shot, and Fire again. Try answering a strike, leaving
a gap or adding a contrasting rhythm. A single well-placed note is welcome.
Only the new ball is physical; the remembered phrase plays once alongside it.

Use the separate mute controls to hear each contribution. Stop, Retry and world
changes cancel obsolete playback. Restore and imports stay silent. Download
recordings or a pairing to retain both physical lines and their captured sound
settings. The separate physical-only export works with older Worldcraft and
Contact Loops, but cannot carry the new acoustic information.

Older Keeps retain their stored gains with canonical voices; their original
timbre may be unknown. Both contributions use the established common sound
delay. A few Descending shots near touching surfaces can change after different
world-loading histories; stored contact recordings remain their own histories.

The listening question is whether the companion gives your next shot a musical
intention. Are separated phrases or clusters easier to answer? Do small changes
offer enough variety? Does the sound feel connected to the physical contact?

Exact tested shots are kept separately in `spatial-solutions.md` and
`counterline-solutions.md`.
