# Bell Loft: endpoint, not a through-chamber

Recommendation only. This note does not change Bell Loft. The numbers are the review on `c080b42` (author `8c2df88`), not a new Havok run.

Keep Bell Loft as an endpoint: a line that can finish upstairs and never touch the ground. A through-chamber, a stage the ball enters and then leaves into a later part of a larger contraption, is a different world. This catalog cannot host it, and turning the loft into one would spend the result the pieces already produce.

## What the geometry already does

The loft is two blocks (top at 4 m), three bell walls, a fourth wall standing aside, and one ramp in front. The ramp pitch is fixed at −20°. Nothing has a height offset. Nothing is a passage through a wall. First ground ends the line. The ball also ends the line when it has been slower than 0.6 m/s for 3 seconds after the 5 second mark.

With the door moved across the opening (x −4, z 49, yaw 0), the opening aim settles on the block at y 4.43 after 15.25 s, position `(0, 4.430, 53.880)`. The same aim’s neighbours settle between 12.4 s and 18.0 s, all at y 4.43. That rest is a destination. The door move is a placement across the gap, not one editor step: nearby slots overlap another piece.

With the door aside, those shots reach the floor and roll out to first ground. Without the three bell walls, the opening takes the stair and the floor and rolls off to first ground at z 67.88. Without the stair it never climbs: first ground at z 32.46, no piece contact. The walls hold the rest in. The ramp is the only way up. An open side is a fall back to the one ground plane, which is the end of the line, not a later stage.

On this branch the receipt can already say the ball came to rest up on the block, and Survey frames that rest. The review of the author commit found the follow camera inside a wall during the flight, and a receipt that said “settled” without saying upstairs. The endpoint is legible after the fact. It is still a slow one: the flat dull top lets the ball crawl, so the catch takes 12–18 seconds to read as finished.

## What a through-chamber would need

Geometry, which this vocabulary does not have. An exit onto another raised surface, at a height the ball can roll to, without dropping to the ground. The block top is at 4 m. The ramp’s height is fixed by its −20° pitch, near 2.1 m, so it cannot be cut to meet that lip. A second block in a row is the same catalog height, and a gap between blocks is a fall to first ground. Overlap is rejected. There is no height offset and no piece the ball can pass through.

Rules, only if the exit is not already a legal continuation. A ball that stays off the ground and keeps its speed is still in play. First ground makes any descent to the yard the end of the shot, so “leave the loft downward” cannot be a stage. Settle makes the successful catch the end of the shot. A through-chamber that pauses in the room would be recorded as finished. Keeping the ball above 0.6 m/s so the line continues is the opposite of the rest this world is built to show. No new goal kind is required for the endpoint. The challenge is already a label.

Presentation, which does not create the exit. The closed door needs the camera and the receipt to show that the ball is upstairs. The open door needs them to show a passage. The review’s in-flight camera still sits in a wall. Fixing that view would make the endpoint easier to see. It would not give the ball a way onward.

So: Bell Loft stays the closed-door rest. A through-chamber waits on a catalog that can place an exit at the loft’s height. This world should not be asked to be both.
