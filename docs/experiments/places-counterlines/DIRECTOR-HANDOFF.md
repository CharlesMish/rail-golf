# Places & Counterlines — director handoff

**Worldcraft can support a collection of distinct activities on the same physical
foundation.** Exchange Yard offers architectural exploration; Counterline makes
an existing physical phrase part of the intention for the next shot. Neither
needs to win a competition to become the whole game.

Owner entry: `/lab/places` on the pinned non-production preview recorded in the
publication ledger and draft PR. [Spoiler-free guide](OWNER-GUIDE.md).
[Repository authority and publication](PUBLICATION.md).

## What this session established

The architectural advance is composition: two low stations, a real crossing,
underpasses, broad receiving roofs and identifiable buildings can support several
different physical intentions within one coherent place. This is more than a
larger rebound box. It is also not evidence that increasing world scale alone
improves play. The new stations individually have fewer sampled contacting shots
than Cistern; their value is the different relationships they expose.

Counterline is an activity change rather than another geometry feature. A kept
phrase plays once beside a new committed Havok shot, preserving its measured
contact times and pitches. The historical layer never becomes a second ball or
an entry in the new shot's contact ledger. The owner's enthusiasm for Contact
Loops justifies this next investigation. It does not yet establish that live
Counterline is enjoyable or that its acoustic buffer feels right.

The corrective lane restores control access at ordinary zoom and offers direct
finite repetition without a mandatory trial. Both old repetition and persistent
object experiments remain available. No new persistent geometry, musical world,
multi-track sequencer or generic authoring schema was needed.

## Architecture: stronger relationships, not universal accessibility

The matched atlas contains 294 explicit legal setups per station/control, not
human play frequencies. Forecourt has 99 contacting shots, Service 155, and
Cistern 164. The complementary stations matter: a low passage toward the dock,
front interception by the crossing, landing on its deck, pitched-roof returns,
lateral hall/platform approaches and gallery/dock relationships differ in
position, incoming direction and consequence. Six principal relationships retain
6/6 sampled neighboring detents. Short shots, extreme yaw and many high arcs
remain unproductive.

There are fewer long tails in this grid: zero Forecourt and two Service shots
exceed 15 seconds, versus 20 Cistern shots. Bounds exits are 29 and 36, versus 58.
This is a bounded comparison, not a claim that every owner shot stays inside or
ends quickly. The dark street retains first-ground termination.

Two failures improved the authorship. The first Service station mostly faced a
near wall. Opening a genuine service court reduced repeated wall interception
while increasing access to the hall, roof and platform—even as total contacts
fell. A later shallow store-roof overhang trapped one line in 24.4 seconds of
clatter and forced an 8–9 m camera reframe. Making that roof flush with its wall
changed the same input to first ground at 13.333 seconds. This also sacrificed a
fragile transfer. Both rejected arrangements remain in the evidence packet.

The final geometric camera audit spans nine actual paths, 7,715 samples, three
viewports and 522 launch views, with no sampled occlusion, inside-solid camera,
camera segment crossing or frustum loss. Maximum post-opening step is 1.23 m at
60 Hz / 2.00 m at 30 Hz. **Rendered camera comfort remains unverified here.**

## Counterline: memory becomes a reason to play

The first study reuses Musical Workshop's existing early-plate arrangement and
offers its original arrangement as a control. The opening three-note phrase
survives 6/8 neighboring changes; the original two-note opening survives 7/8.
The coarse 100-shot grids nevertheless contain 70 and 90 silent outcomes. These
are useful local musical relationships, not a broadly accessible instrument.
A dedicated musical world should wait until owner play tells us what contrast,
rests or answering phrases are actually wanted.

One audio clock establishes the launch origin; Havok remains at 120 Hz and runs
every due step. Both parts use a deliberate 100 ms acoustic buffer. A 20 ms
buffer loses headroom at the sampled 30 Hz partition; 50 ms fails the mixed
partition's 70 ms frame burst. This justifies the initial engineering choice,
not an assertion that 100 ms is musically ideal. Excessive delay, suspension or
focus loss interrupts explicitly rather than silently drifting or dropping time.

Live completion lets a longer companion and tails finish. Stop, Retry, another
launch, changing source/world and leaving retire obsolete audio. Reload/restore
never starts playback. Saved pairings contain two independent physical lines and
explicit canonical voice/gain/mute/delay settings. Older records lack some voice
selection details; exact historical timbre is not promised. A mid-performance
mute change prevents saving that audition as a fixed mix while leaving the new
physical line Keepable.

Actual Havok paths, contacts, terminal results and resonance performance match
Solo exactly at equivalent 15/30/60/144 Hz and mixed partitions. Browser audio
measurement separately rendered the actual voices and scheduled six recorded
notifications at their intended times with no late events in that run. It is
explicitly **not** live browser Havok verification: WebGL was unavailable.

## Director recommendation

Prioritize **bounded architectural composition with differentiated launch
stations** and **musical line memory used during live play**. The former deserves
a small approved catalog of solid slabs, roofs and supports only after more
authored worlds show which shapes recur. The latter can remain an optional
activity over the current Worldcraft line format, with separate pairing records;
it does not require changing physical world identity or imported scripts.

Invite the next independent author to make a compact, unscored musical works
where two approachable physical regions offer contrasting short phrases and
useful silence. Give the author the reliable current pieces and Counterline,
without prescribing a melody or contact count. First obtain owner feedback on
this existing-world integration; otherwise a new layout risks solving the wrong
musical problem.

Change this recommendation if Exchange Yard still feels like a handful of
solutions in a mostly inaccessible scene, if camera interruptions outweigh the
journeys, or if accompaniment does not affect the intended next shot. If the
100 ms delay feels detached, latency and scheduling deserve attention before any
extra layer. For persistent objects, the next meaningful question is a purposeful
delivery channel or slope, not further tuning of the same flat garden. Preserve
both stone and sphere until that authored context provides evidence.

Technically possible and reproducibly authorable are demonstrated here.
Discoverability and expressive possibilities have supporting local evidence.
Enjoyment of these new activities remains the owner's decision.
