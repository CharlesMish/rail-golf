# A first visit: playability, persistence and repetition

Open `/lab/playability` on the pinned non-production preview linked from the new draft PR. The page displays its build. The original Next Playgrounds remains available for comparison; Theatre is unchanged. Nothing here changes production.

## Gallery Cistern — watch a journey

Begin at the launcher. Choose aim, elevation, power and rail position as usual. The blue gallery is high, the ochre gallery leads toward the side chamber, and the green landing is low. The dark floor and launch pier end a shot.

Use **Survey** before firing to understand the room, then return to the launch view. During flight, **Follow** stays with the actual projectile; **Overview** gives you the larger room. These change your view, never the trajectory. **Stop** ends the attempt when you have seen enough, and is explicitly recorded as interruption rather than a landing. Retry prepares another shot.

Try a region you are curious about. After the first interesting route, ask whether you want to reach another part of the room, return by a different path, or shorten a journey. An escape is possible: this is an open-topped room within a larger study boundary. The ending message identifies the boundary or terminal surface.

The revised camera has numerical visibility checks, but could not be rendered in the research browser because WebGL was unavailable. Its comfort and clarity in your browser are an important part of this visit.

## Rolling Yard — make a useful change

The boulder and the original-shaped stone are separate comparisons. Choose one, then aim a shot at the object or a surface that could approach it differently. The cradle and side alcove suggest places to put it; neither is compulsory or scored.

Ordinary continuation is **Fire → watch → let the object settle → Fire again**. The new Fire acts on the altered yard. You do not need to approve a second result screen before continuing. Painted markings make the boulder's actual rotation visible.

Undo goes back one blow. Reset puts the selected object home. Keep preserves a chosen blow, including its original starting arrangement and settled result. Restore before prepares the original state; Use result prepares its settled outcome. Both leave firing to you. Changing the object starts its separate comparison rather than converting a kept stone into a sphere.

A useful question: does the extra rolling help you place it, or does the chunky stone give you better control? Try a second intention after moving it once.

## Shot Repeater — an unchanged launch meets a changed world

First fire a manual trial. When the stone has settled, choose a finite sequence of three to five shots with that same setup. **Start** continues from the stone's current position. It does not put the world back at the trial's beginning.

Each shot is a fresh Havok attempt. Only one projectile is active, and the next waits for the stone to settle. Watch whether identical settings keep doing useful work, strike a different face, or begin to miss. Each attempt has its own record; the sequence is not one historical line.

**Stop** cancels the remaining launches while allowing the already committed shot and settling to finish. Reset and historical Restore do not start a sequence. Leaving or hiding the page cancels future firing; it never resumes automatically when you return.

A useful question: can you choose an action that remains useful as its own work changes the world? It is fine to stop after discovering why a repeated setup fails.

## Contact Loops — listen to history

This is **audio-only playback of recorded physical contacts**. No new projectile, collision or world change occurs while it plays.

Choose two recorded lines, begin with either layer alone, then listen together. The sources retain their measured contact timing. The second layer can enter half a shared cycle later; this is an arrangement choice, not a different physical shot. Stop ends playback, and each listen is finite.

Measured example lines provide a starting listen. You can also use supported resonant Worldcraft Keeps from the same browser origin, or import a Worldcraft memory export from another preview. Silent or unsupported records remain untouched. Saving a pair saves sources and arrangement; loading never starts sound.

A useful question: does layering reveal a rhythm you want to keep, or mostly repeat a collision you have already heard? The source histories are available separately from the invitation, so no successful launch recipe is required to begin listening.
