# Instrument Foundry — Independent Exhibition

**Admit all three originals, unchanged, beside the two references.** Exchange,
Hairpin and Antiphonal Arc offer sufficiently different physical material to
justify an owner audition. This is temporary exhibition admission, not permanent
repertoire selection. No revised instrument, new geometry capability or shared
physical/acoustic engine change was needed.

Start with [the spoiler-free play guide](OWNER-PLAY.md). The exhibition route is
`/lab/instrument-exhibition`. Exact published source, draft PR and pinned hosted
preview are recorded in [the publication ledger](PUBLICATION.md) and its linked

ancestor kit, `3c67ceb9de1739c23092f6fdd8680b9047536a9f`.

## What this round establishes

Independent authors used one bounded vocabulary to make three recognizable
physical activities. Exchange leaves space between gestures. Hairpin makes the
same two surfaces speak repeatedly, with power changing their timing. Arc adds
vertical routes to its lateral court. Those are useful differences without a
shared contact-count target. A single striking note remains a complete contribution.

The technical evidence is strongest for reproducible local neighborhoods, source
preservation and ensemble independence. Broad samples expose substantial silent
regions. They do not measure human discovery rates. Actual listening and rendered
owner exploration must decide whether these activities invite another shot.

Exchange and Arc were also measured on the **same** 180-setting domain. Arc gives
46 multi-impact outcomes versus Exchange's 12; that is not a ranking of instruments.
Removing Arc's canopy reduces its multi-impact count to 34 and eliminates its
characteristic vertical replies, while leaving all ten sampled lateral crossings.
The canopy adds another physical relationship; it does not improve every lateral
shot. Removing the wings preserves the vertical core but removes lateral replies.
All twelve Arc pieces are reached across the common sample and a separate loft
fan. The controls have separate review-only identities and are not exhibited as
author revisions. Hairpin's different grid cannot be used in this comparison.

## Authorship, acoustics and recorded companions

The original six instrument/probe files are byte-identical to the intake. Names,
ordered geometry, original and curated openings, pitches, profiles, invitations
and cameras are preserved. The complete original package, including author
archives, surrogate evidence, images, negative experiments and intake review,
is retained byte-for-byte as `original-intake.zip`. New evidence is separately
named. No author script was executed as verification authority.

The exhibition is a separate validated collection, not a change to the ordinary
Foundry registry. Descending Resonance and Chime Terraces remain the exact
references. The shared player supplies Keep/Restore, silent import, world changes,
recording, export and solo/duet/trio. A companion remains music from its captured
world; selecting it does not restore that world. Three occupied exhibition slots
leave three of the existing additional-instrument slots available for visit imports.
Four Keeps and four saved ensembles retain the existing limits and formats.

All three submissions use the captured `normal-speed` / `tuned-even` interpretation.
The latter name is narrower than its behavior: it applies the even contact-gain
curve to all voiced contacts. Only timber banks receive the specialized tuned
timber voice, trim and narrower gain range. Hairpin's ramp/plate timbres stay
canonical. We clarified this in the UI and reports without changing sound.

Repeated actual impacts remain musical evidence even when they add no sport
redirect. Hairpin's central five impacts can have two qualified redirects; a
same-step seam contact can sound two pitches at the same timestamp. Neither is
discarded or relabeled as an extra separated bounce. Hairpin's re-voiced gain
arrays differ from the submitted final floating-point bits in 64/158 records,
at most 1.11e-16; physical payloads, pitches and timestamps remain exact.

Twenty-two independently generated actual-Havok source recordings retain complete
physical snapshots and acoustic sidecars. Twenty-seven solo/duet/trio comparisons
across three render-frame partitions produce identical complete physical payloads
under equivalent stepping. Only one projectile enters physics. The 280 selected
cross-instrument combinations reach at most 15 overlapping attacks; a deliberate
three-copy dense Hairpin stress case reaches 30. Keep the existing 36-attack bound.
It refuses unsupported density explicitly; no sampled trio needs an expansion.

Actual browser OfflineAudioContext measurements give mixed float peaks of .1335
(sparse trio), .2197 (independent rhythmic trio), .3429 (dense Hairpin stress) and
.1697 (reference answer) at the default layer levels. Dense Hairpin at all layers'
permitted maximum reaches .5098. These examples do not clip; they do not establish
perceived balance or bound all possible future mixtures. Actual recorded-clock
runs schedule 14 and 30 attacks with no late events and zero active source nodes
after natural completion. These are recorded-notification tests, not rendered
live Havok synchronization or a listening verdict.

## Replay, camera and resource boundaries

Exchange's selected 36 prior-world comparisons, Hairpin's 114 and Arc's selected
35 match complete fresh physical histories. A stronger Arc canopy-gap challenge
finds **4/20 different complete physical outcomes** after Hairpin → Terraces → Arc.
Three land .379–.618 m away from their fresh controls; one ends a physics tick later
and changes qualified redirects from one to zero. The fourth differs by less than
.001 m. All four retain identical sounding pieces, pitches, onsets and gains.
Recorded playback remains the immutable captured performance. Re-firing its setup
is a separate simulation, and universal physical equivalence is not established.

Arc's positive box separation does not prevent a .86 m projectile bridging a
roughly .504 m supporting-axis gap. This is measured multiple-body contact
sensitivity, not a proved solver root cause. Preserve the counterexample before
considering a separately identified revision. Do not secretly alter the canopy
or the shared engine for its first exhibition.

Runtime-only static-body/mesh counts are Exchange 7/7, Hairpin 14/24 and Arc 14/20,
against Descending 14/20 and Terraces 23/41. Each live shot adds one body.
Measured reset/disposal cycles return counted resources to their expected levels
and then zero. Warm NullEngine builds around a few milliseconds are CPU observations
under concurrent research load; they are neither draw-call nor GPU/phone/frame
performance measurements. Individual reports retain the actual resource scope.

The research browser cannot initialize WebGL. This round verifies real browser
audio and scrollable controls at four CSS viewport sizes, but **not** rendered
worlds, occlusion, shadows, physical-phone performance or audible enjoyment.
Geometric framing predicts that Overview contains the full Exchange/Arc layouts;
their opposite receivers can be clipped in initial Launch views. Hairpin's author
captures show its gap clearly in Overview and dark lid undersides. These are useful
owner checks, not new rendered passes. Browser download delivery remains unverified
after an automation timeout; format/source round-trips pass automated checks.

## Director recommendation

First audition the unchanged collection. Try a short retained gesture against a
different instrument before choosing which deserves refinement. Exchange's few
pieces should not be filled in to resemble Arc; Hairpin's recurring pitches should
not be filtered to resemble sport recognition; Arc should not be graded against
surrogate promises that actual Havok disproved.

No new generic geometry, synthesis engine or register expansion is justified by
this intake. Hairpin's reported failed curves, funnels and traps are useful author
history, but their discarded prototype files were not supplied for reproduction.
The most valuable future engineering question is ball-scale clearance and
construction-history behavior around adjacent faces. The most valuable musical
question is whether the owner keeps complementary short material across worlds.

Owner evidence that would change this recommendation: repeated inability to find
a second useful gesture in an instrument; unintelligible camera occlusion; a dense
phrase that consistently masks companions; or a specific missing shape/register
needed for an otherwise compelling physical intention. Any later correction gets
a new identity and preserves this original exhibition as its control.
