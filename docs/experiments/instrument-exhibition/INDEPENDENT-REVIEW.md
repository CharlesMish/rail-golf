### Arc construction-history qualification

The selected ordinary Arc families match in 35/35 history comparisons. A targeted
20-setting canopy-gap fan is a stronger challenge: four complete physical hashes
differ after Hairpin, Terraces and Arc are built/played in sequence. Three land
approximately .379–.618 m differently; one ends one physics tick later and changes
qualified redirects from one to zero. The fourth differs by less than .001 m.
All retain first-ground termination and **exact voiced piece/note order, onset
times and gains** in these samples. This is a material physical difference, not
just floating-point formatting, but it is not a changed musical phrase here.

The recorded sources remain immutable actual histories. Replaying their audio is
different from firing their setup again. The evidence does not support promising
universal identical physical re-firing around simultaneous canopy contacts.
Preserve the original and disclose the boundary; do not repair the shared solver
or secretly alter its canopy before the owner's first audition.

## Acoustic and ensemble review

The original `normal-speed`/`tuned-even` profiles are consistent with the engine,
but the `bankVoice` field name understates their scope. The even 18 dB gain curve
applies to non-bank impacts too. Tuned timber timbre, its trim and narrower 12 dB
curve apply only to timber banks. Hairpin's ramp/plate timbres remain canonical.
This review caught a bank-only implication in the Exchange report; that wording
was corrected without retuning anything. The player now explains the distinction.

Hairpin's independently recomputed playback gains differ from supplied final bits
in 64/158 runs, at most `1.1102230246251565e-16`. Physical histories, voiced event
structure, pitches and times match exactly. Keeping separate exact and tolerant
acoustic comparisons is appropriate; relaxing the physical hash check would not be.

The ensemble reviewer generates 22 actual-Havok sources and compares 27 complete
live payloads across zero/one/two companions and three render-frame partitions.
Every physical payload matches its solo control and only one dynamic projectile
exists. The fake-clock scheduler preserves original times plus the common 100 ms
delay. Four recorded fixtures finish with 5, 14, 30 and 12 attacks respectively.
The 280 selected cross-instrument combinations peak at 15 overlapping conservative
lifetimes; a deliberately dense three-copy Hairpin stress fixture reaches 30.
Neither result justifies increasing the existing 36-attack limit.

The synthetic 37th-notification test explicitly interrupts and cleans up; it is
not an actual physical phrase. Fake contexts/node ledgers establish parameters
and ownership, not audible balance, browser latency or real node allocation.
All 22 recordings retain separate physical snapshots/acoustic sidecars, and four
arrangements round-trip through the bounded library without rewriting old records.
Removal from a temporary catalog does not remove those source snapshots.

The evidence suggests useful material contrast: space between Exchange replies,
compact Hairpin repetitions and Arc's vertical/material changes. These are
possibilities to listen for, not compulsory musical roles. A sparse metal strike
may remain more prominent than several soft timber contacts. Preserve the authored
profiles and adjustable layers until the owner has heard their combinations.

## Verification and limits

This review's unchanged PR92 baseline passed the production build, all **720
repository tests**, TypeScript and lint with **zero errors/five inherited
warnings**. After the player patch, **49 focused tests** passed across instrument
admission/provenance, restore policy, Counterline, repertoire and ensemble suites.
The director's final integrated checks are separate and must be reported at their
actual tested source, rather than substituting this baseline result.

Runtime reset/disposal and source independence are well covered by the other
lanes. These are counted NullEngine bodies/meshes/materials and fake audio ownership;
they do not establish GPU memory, draw calls, physical-phone performance or smooth
rendering. The review inspected two supplied Hairpin software-renderer captures;
these are author evidence, not new WebGL inspection. Geometric camera estimates
cannot establish actual occlusion, shadows or usability. Owner listening and
rendered exploration remain necessary, especially the far receiver from Exchange's
portrait launch view and Hairpin's roof/lid occlusion.

Arc seam/re-firing limitation must remain visible in the technical handoff. The
first owner session should compare original instruments before proposing geometry,
register, synthesis or universal authoring changes. Hairpin's unavailable discarded
curve experiments are useful reported design history, not reproducible evidence
that a new generic geometry feature is required.
