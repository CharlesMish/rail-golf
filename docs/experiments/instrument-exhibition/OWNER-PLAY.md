# A short visit to the Instrument Exhibition

The exhibition contains Descending Resonance, Chime Terraces, Exchange, Hairpin
and Antiphonal Arc. The three submissions retain their original geometry,
acoustics, camera settings and authored openings. Inclusion is an invitation to
try them, not a decision about the permanent repertoire.

1. Choose an instrument in **Musical world**. Use **Starting setup** for its
   author's suggested entry, or **Original opening** for its underlying opening.
   Loading either is silent. Try **Overview** when a receiving surface is hidden.
2. Fire solo, then change one control at a time. A single good strike or a short
   answer is worth Keeping; a longer phrase is not automatically better.
3. Keep something you like and choose **Companion 1**. Switch the live instrument
   and answer it. The recorded source stays selected; only your new shot enters
   physics. Choose a second retained source for a trio, or None for solo play.
4. Adjust levels before Fire. Mute is available during playback; Stop cancels it.
   Let a pairing finish naturally before saving a fixed ensemble. Changing the
   mix during a performance prevents saving that performance as a fixed mix.
5. Download a useful line or ensemble, then try importing it. Import/loading is
   silent. **Restore physical setup** is a separate choice from selecting music.
   Four Keeps and four saved ensembles remain bounded; export before removing
   anything. Saved ensembles retain their own independent source copies.

**Exchange:** explore how a small gesture can leave room for another instrument.
Try a short answer as well as a continuation. Its openness is part of the design.

**Hairpin:** discover the space between the timber floor and the metal lid.
Listen for changes in pulse, then try making a shorter contribution. Overview
can help you understand a turn that the lid hides from the launch camera.

**Antiphonal Arc:** compare the two wings with the space above them. A direct
strike is a valid beginning. Its original opening is preserved even where the
author's predicted reply did not occur in our actual-Havok reproduction.

For the first visit, avoid the technical recipes. Afterwards, the most useful
feedback is one exported phrase you wanted to Keep, one adjustment that suggested
the next shot, and anything that made the instrument hard to see or understand.
Try a sparse companion alongside a busier gesture before stacking several busy
ones. There is no assigned musical role or required number of notes.

The cloud research browser could not render WebGL; owner inspection of launch
and Overview cameras is still needed. Arc also has a documented near-canopy-gap
re-firing limitation: some measured landings depend on prior world construction.
Its retained musical recordings remain independent, immutable source histories.
Details and numerical probes are in the technical reports, not this invitation.
