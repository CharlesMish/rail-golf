# Graduated Rail Rat — future study protocol

**Design only. Do not run during the Linecraft commission.**

Question: does a short sentence curriculum make subsequent Open exploration
more specific and diverse, or merely bias players toward its demonstrated routes?
The comparison is not a contest between KEEP, SENTENCE and SCORE as products.
Keep is available to both arms throughout.

## Participants and assignment

Use fresh participants or fresh, independent Rail Rat contexts with no previous
Rail Golf sessions, solution documents, authoring fixtures or reviewer outputs.
Record human versus agent, model/version when applicable, input/device, browser,
viewport and prior relevant game experience. Do not pool those populations as if
their control learning and motives were interchangeable.

Randomly assign matched participants to:

- **LEARN → OPEN:** shared motor warmup, three sentence blocks, then Open.
- **COLD OPEN → OPEN:** the same motor warmup and station exposure, with free Open
  play instead of sentences, then the same final Open block.

Use a between-participant comparison. A sentence-trained participant cannot
become cold again by clearing storage, changing the label or opening a new tab.
Charlie's familiar-yard play remains useful owner evidence but belongs outside
the fresh-participant comparison.

Record all assigned participants, including those who never complete a sentence.
Do not compare only successful graduates with all cold players. Report exposure
and completion descriptively without treating successful completion as randomized.

## Fixed budget: 46 launched shots, approximately 35–50 minutes

| Block | Both arms | LEARN arm | COLD OPEN arm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motor warmup | Four launches: two at Gate, two at Walk; up to five minutes. | Same neutral controls briefing. | Same neutral controls briefing. |
| Exposure 1 | Six launches at Gate. | BANK A → BANK B. | Open, no required sentence. |
| Exposure 2 | Six launches at Walk. | TREAD 1 → TREAD 2. | Open, no required sentence. |
| Exposure 3 | Six launches at Walk. | TREAD 1 → TREAD 2 → TREAD 3. | Open, no required sentence. |
| Transfer Open | Twenty-four launches: twelve at each station. | Required sentence removed. | Same Open presentation. |
| Return check | Up to five minutes; no required new launches. | Same optional Shelf inspection. | Same optional Shelf inspection. |

Use the same shot ceilings and time ceilings in both arms. Stop a block at its
ceiling and retain the incomplete result; do not give extra attempts until a
preferred outcome appears. Suggested exposure ceiling is fifteen minutes total,
and transfer ceiling twenty minutes. If control difficulty consumes a ceiling,
report that as an outcome and include actual opportunity/time in comparisons.

On early sentence completion, explicitly acknowledge it and let the participant
use the remaining station block freely, without prescribing another route.
Record which launches actually had a prompt. The assigned opportunity remains
equal even when successful players receive more unprompted time. The six-launch
budgets are study limits, not a claim that every novice ought to solve a sentence
in six attempts.

Count every initiated launch, including an interrupted Retry, toward the budget.
Separate abort/interrupt from a resolved miss and sentence failure. Technical
failures are recorded separately; replay a failed technical block only under a
predeclared rule, never because its exploration looked uninteresting.

Counterbalance the station order in the final transfer block within each arm:
half Gate → Walk, half Walk → Gate. This makes total station exposure identical
while balancing the initial opportunity after the final Walk lesson. Main
comparison uses the same station totals; first-six-shot transfer is also reported
by starting station. The curriculum's own Gate → Walk order remains fixed.

## Shared briefing and motor warmup

Teach only how to aim, adjust elevation/power, change rails, Fire, Survey, Retry,
Restore and Keep, plus the unchanged first-ground ending. Identify the two station
controls. Demonstrate UI operation, not a successful route, and do not supply
numeric solutions or a predicted trajectory.

Each participant gets the same four low-stakes launches. They may vary controls
and observe outcomes. Do not ask them to hit particular banks, complete tread
orders, reverse a route or collect claims. Explain that historical ghosts are
recorded paths, and that Restore requires a manual Fire.

Use the same authored station defaults and initial Kicker A state for both arms.
These opening views/aims may already make some relationships more accessible;
document that shared assistance instead of calling the controls completely
unguided. Keep the same camera and labels except for the treatment's sentence
instruction and clause feedback.

## Score presentation and recovery controls

For the first study, hide score in **both arms for every block**, while retaining
the frozen score receipt in local evidence. This limits score-seeking as a
competing explanation for transfer. Do not change claim values, recognition,
precedence, RUN, VARIETY, physics or the first-ground rule.

A later independent comparison may randomize score shown/hidden during Open.
Do not mix that extra treatment into this small initial comparison or let
participants switch it unnoticed. If a participant changes visibility, record the
fire-time state and report the deviation; do not discard the shot silently.

Retry, exact Restore, Survey and previous-history visibility are equally available.
Use identical fresh-block reset rules in both arms: station's authored opening
setup, Kicker A, timed power and MAX off. Within a block, ordinary Retry retains
its incumbent semantics; Restore reinstates the recorded starting environment.
Reset transfer history/aim presentation equally at entry while preserving earned
Shelf lines and the immutable study log. This prevents an automatically displayed
last taught shot from becoming the first Open prompt; voluntary Shelf use remains
part of the behavior under study.

## Novelty protections

- No source code, test fixtures, QA coordinates, exact reference powers, author
  videos, complete target solutions, score-farming examples or other participants'
  outputs enter the tester context.
- Teach only A→B and the tread pair/triple. **Do not mention B→A, reversal,
  combination, truncation or a “novelty challenge” in the Open prompt.** Those are
  analyst categories; mentioning them would seed the intended outcomes.
- Neutral Open prompt: “The yard is open. There is no required sentence. Decide
  what you want to try.” Feature identity may remain visible; an answer sequence
  must not replace the removed prompt.
- Do not reveal novelty scoring or reward novelty with points/badges. Additional
  recognition families are outside this study.
- Use the same stations, start state, control help and opportunity budget. No
  instructor intervention after a miss beyond neutral controls clarification.
- Preserve unfamiliar feature interactions and bank reversal as untaught
  transfer opportunities. Failure to discover a particular hidden possibility is
  not itself failure of learning.
- Make Keep available with the same four-slot limit and no score requirement in
  both arms. Record when the Shelf is full; post-cap absence of Keeps is censored,
  not evidence that later lines lacked value.

## What to record

Before each measured Open launch, ask for one short intention: “What do you want
this shot to do?” Use the same question in both arms. It should not suggest route
families or demand novelty. After resolution, ask at most “What would you change
next?” Do not interrupt every shot with a long questionnaire.

Join the following by immutable attempt ID:

- assigned arm, block, station, shot index, active prompt and score visibility;
- authored stage exposure, deepest reached clause, complete/incomplete and
  deliberate Continue/skip;
- exact setup/environment/build, ordered evidence and terminal;
- original score receipt and recognized events, computed by unchanged authority;
- Keep decision and its time, Shelf-full opportunity, Restore/retry lineage and
  deliberate Ghost/replay use;
- pre-shot intention, requested next change, interruptions, technical failures and
  neutral help received.

“Not kept” must remain separate from “explicitly rejected.” Normal Retry with no
decision is an unobserved choice, not a negative preference. A line can be kept
for humor, beauty or personal meaning despite low score or no recognized events.

## Analysis plan

The primary qualitative comparison is whether final Open intentions name a
specific physical relationship and subsequent adjustments pursue it. Report the
distribution by participant and station, not just a pooled shot count.

Independent reviewers, blind to arm and numerical score where feasible, classify
the stated intention together with the observed line:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Exact taught repetition | A taught required relationship repeated with no stated material departure. Incidental extra contact alone does not prove a new intention. |
| Taught-route revision | Deliberate improvement or targeted clause repair within the taught relationship. Useful fluency, but not transfer novelty. |
| Deliberate transformation | A player-originated change in order, subset, combination, endpoint or relationship supported by the pre-shot intention. |
| Untaught relationship | A specific intended relationship outside the required curriculum. |
| Control exploration | A purposeful test of power/elevation/origin or rebound behavior without a route-level intention. Legitimate early learning. |
| Undirected or unclear | Intention does not identify what the player is testing, or evidence is insufficient to classify. |

Reviewers may use the event ledger and sampled trail but must not equate a claim
ID with a geometric family. Different geometry can yield the same recognized
sequence; an unrecognized physical departure can still matter. Distinguish
**attempted novel intention** from **successful novel execution**. An accidental
new sequence counts as an outcome, not automatically as deliberate transfer.

Secondary summaries: distinct intended/observed relationships per equal shot
opportunity; repairs that target the failed clause; station switching/Survey and
Restore use; voluntary Keep rate before the cap; later viewing/restoring of kept
lines; descriptive agreement/disagreement between frozen score and Keep.

Do not optimize score from these data. Keep is not a truth label for enjoyment,
recognition is not the full space of possible lines, and a small pilot does not
warrant a causal product verdict. A promising result is specific, varied
self-direction without a dominant exact-repetition effect, accompanied by
credible controls learning. A neutral or negative result can motivate a better
curriculum, improved legibility, or a different open-play frame; it does not
automatically justify new geometry.

## Delayed memory check and limits

Optionally invite the same participants back after several days with no required
shot challenge. Ask what, if anything, they want to revisit before showing their
Shelf. Record voluntary viewing, Restore/manual firing and desire to share.
This is a separate delayed observation, not tonight's experiment and not proof
of long-term retention.

Known limits include the small two-family syllabus, authored opening aim,
asymmetric Gate/Walk exposure within the curriculum, verbal intention reporting
changing behavior, four-slot Keep censoring, browser/rendering reliability, and
agent reasoning that may not resemble human motor learning. Publishing the
protocol before running it preserves a distinction between teaching vocabulary
and selecting favorable answers afterward.
