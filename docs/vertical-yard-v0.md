# Vertical Yard v0 — isolated spatial study

Route: `/lab/vertical-yard`. Parent authority: accepted Mechanism Range v0.3, `354c4084fcf12cfed037ba3d341989edef61d07e`.

## Spatial thesis

An elevated hoist apron looks into one open timber handling hall. Low returns, a sequence of rising storage terraces, an upper gallery and a leaning far receiver share the same void. The experiment asks whether choosing an **altitude and return level** produces understandable lines, before adding another machine or a score economy.

This is a blockout, not a finished second course. There is one station, three incumbent rails and no target, score, progression, wind or switch. The spherical body, gravity, mass, launch speed, aim limits and charge curve remain the existing Rail Golf authorities. RAIL ROUND and familiar launcher-relative framing are defaults. The only launch-origin change is the authored apron at eight metres above the lower yard.

## Three spatial regions

| Region | Geometry | Intended invitation |
|---|---|---|
| Lower return yard | Angled timber cheek and broad, shallow ramp beneath the upper structures | Descend into a usable surface; distinguish a low continuation from a ground-ending miss. |
| Rising storage / upper gallery | Right terraces at 6, 14 and 22 m; left gallery at 18 m; cross gantry at 32 m | Choose under, beside or over a mass. The gaps between terraces remain open rather than forming a staircase solution. |
| High receiver | Broad timber plate leaning 18° over the far void, centred near 113 m | Reach a high face whose ordinary normal can send the round down and back into the lower space. |

All broad faces and visible support posts have actual static Havok bodies. The far plate has a pale inset face and dark edge strips to expose its normal at distance. Surface trim and floor work rails are visual details, not hidden catchers. Physical extents and mesh extents derive from the same records.

The lower floor and launch apron end the line on contact. Other authored solids can rebound under ordinary restitution (the same .86 / .18 surface pair used by Mechanism Range timber). Meaningful OOB remains on every outer side; the receiver does not enclose the yard. Existing generic qualification may caption an honest departure but there is no new recognition predicate or reward.

## Route hypotheses — not solutions

- A lower cheek departure may enter the central ramp or traverse toward the rising terraces.
- A terrace side and a terrace top may be different ways to enter the same upper void.
- A high shot can approach the far plate above or below the gantry; the resulting downward leg may pass under the gallery or reach the lower ramp.
- The open space below the gallery may be more useful than its top. A player may discover a line involving its edge or honest support post that was not authored as a route.
- The far receiver may prove too easy to reach directly, or too hard to combine with another surface. It is not intended to guarantee a multi-contact line.

QA coordinates remain in source fixtures, not in page copy. A feasible physical fixture establishes that a line exists; it does not establish that the invitation is readable or enjoyable.

## Deliberately unknown

The eight-metre apron introduces a useful downward destination while keeping familiar equipment framing, but whether this improves player intuition needs a human pass. The current minimum elevation is still the incumbent +5°, so lower surfaces are reached ballistically rather than with a new downward-aim control. No special allowance was added.

No rendered visual quality or discoverability claim is established by NullEngine projection/contact checks. In particular, the far plate and gantry may overlap too much from the launch view, and the right terraces may demand Survey before their height steps are clear. This is the main manual question, not something to compensate for with labels or stronger recognition.

## First manual five minutes

1. Look from launch and Survey: can the upper/lower alternatives be read without instruction?
2. Try a familiar low-power line and a high line. Does the raised apron feel like the same launcher in a different place?
3. Attempt any far contact, then vary height to see whether its return enters another region.
4. Note every solid-looking surface that fails to behave physically as expected.
5. Decide whether verticality warrants another iteration before adding any mechanism, target or points.

## Established physical authority

Eight focused tests use the same configured session as the browser and real Havok. They establish static face transforms, the eight-metre launch offset with incumbent aim/impulse, launcher clearance on all three rails, four different cross-region sequences, repeated-shot agreement, browser-observable stepping agreement, recovery/Recall, and familiar camera projection in desktop/mobile aspect ratios.

A coarse exploratory scan of 540 setups found 98 records containing multiple qualified departures or a far-receiver departure, spanning 28 ordered families. This is feasibility evidence, not a balance or discoverability survey. The retained regression uses nine nearby high-return setups across all three rails: each rebounds from the gantry into the far face above 35 m, then travels downward/backward into the lower yard. Separate fixtures traverse lower cheek → ramp → terrace, ramp → middle/upper terrace, gallery → cheek → gantry, and ramp → upper terrace → far return. No body imparts a destination-aware impulse.

The high receiver is not a safety net: a tested faster top-edge contact still exits the authored bounds. No fixture promises a completed return-to-another-face after the high receiver; the demonstrated return enters lower **space** and reaches the ground. Whether players can deliberately compose a further surface interaction is left open.

NullEngine checks verify the familiar muzzle remains centred and inside the frame at 1440×900, 844×390 and 390×844. They do not replace a rendered visual inspection.
